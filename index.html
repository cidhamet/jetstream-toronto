The gap we are trying to close

AI safety has built a clear path for researchers. It has not built one for the people who run everything else.

Organizations working on AI safety struggle to hire for operations, programmes, recruiting and communications. Plenty of capable people want that work, but a CV cannot show whether someone can take a vague problem and ship something useful.

Jet Stream gives them five days to show it: a real problem, a deadline, a mentor and an audience.

If you want to do AI safety research, the route is well marked: a course, a mentored project, a fellowship, a job. By Kairos's count, more than 20 research fellowships will train between 2,000 and 2,500 people this year. For everything else an organization needs, roughly seven programmes will train about 300.

Meanwhile the work piles up. 80,000 Hours reports that organizations working on AI safety struggle to fill roles such as chief of staff, recruiting lead and programme manager. A review of the evidence from April 2026 found that hiring for operational and fieldbuilding roles is challenging across the field.

Interest is not the problem. A non-research posting can draw hundreds of applications and still surface only zero to five people who meet the core requirements. One organization counted 670 applications for a single special-projects role. A CV cannot show whether someone can take a vague problem and ship something useful.

  • 670applications one organization received for a single special-projects roleIsmaili and Linnik, July 2026

How the week works

You choose a project before you arrive, from a list of real problems sourced from AI safety organizations. Then you build. Every day ends with something concrete in hand.

  1. BeforeFrom late December

    Find your starting point

    Join an opening call, read a short list of background material, and rank your top three projects. Arrive in Toronto on Thursday 7 January.

    You start withA matched project and mentor

  2. Fri 8 JanDay 1

    Scope and pitch

    Get oriented, then cut the project down to something you can finish. Agree the plan with your mentor and pitch it to the cohort by the end of the day.

    You end the day withA scoped plan, pitched

  3. Sat 9 JanDay 2

    First build

    One long build block. The bar for the day is simple: something exists that did not exist before, however rough.

    You end the day withA rough first version

  4. Sun 10 JanDay 3

    Red team

    Someone else pokes holes in your weakest assumption, and you check in with your mentor. This is the last real chance to change direction.

    You end the day withA tested, adjusted plan

  5. Mon 11 JanDay 4

    Finish and package

    A final build block in the morning. The afternoon shifts to packaging: a short demo video, a one-pager, an outreach draft and a handover note.

    You end the day withA demo video and a one-pager

  6. Tue 12 JanDay 5

    Demo day

    Send your work to at least one person who can use it, finalize your 30-day plan, and demo in front of the cohort and guests from AI safety organizations.

    You leave withWork in the right hands and a 30-day plan

The point is not to finish an entire project in five days. It is to get far enough that the work can be judged, shared, continued or dropped. After the week we check in at 30 and 90 days, and make introductions where there is a fit.

Who it's for

Mainly mid-career professionals moving into AI safety, but there is no fixed career stage or experience bar. We look for initiative, the ability to work independently, and skills worth showing.

  • Mid-career professionals looking to move into AI safety
  • BlueDot and SPAR graduates
  • AI safety group organizers who have already shown initiative
  • Early-career people and graduate students with relevant experience
  • People who have built or organized something useful on their own

What to expect

  • A week of doing, not a course. Very few talks.
  • You should already have some AI safety context before you arrive.
  • Open to people across North America.

Have a project? Bring it.

For organizations, the week works like an extended work trial. You get progress on a problem you care about and a close look at how someone works. It takes about two hours of your time, and you can mentor remotely.

  1. Submit a projectOne form per project. We review on a rolling basis until 15 December 2026.
  2. Get matched with a fellowFellows rank the projects they want. We confirm matches in late December.
  3. Two 30-minute callsA kickoff on day 1 and a check-in on day 3, with an optional debrief on day 4. The sprint runs over a weekend, so we can move calls if that suits you better.
  4. Demo day, then decideSee the result on Tuesday 12 January. Nothing is binding: you choose afterwards whether to keep working together.

What makes a good project

  • One capable generalist can finish it, or move it meaningfully, in about 20 focused hours.
  • The output will be used, not filed.
  • You can say what done looks like on demo day.
  • Ideally you can offer a next step to a strong fellow: paid work, a trial, or a warm introduction.
